# Buy Nothing Project — Developer Volunteer & Internship Learning Program

Thanks for your interest in the Buy Nothing Project's Developer Volunteer & Internship Learning Program. This document explains the program's purpose, how it's structured, and what the experience looks like. Read it end-to-end before applying — it'll help you decide whether the fit is right.

The program supports two pathways under a single learning framework:

- **Volunteer pathway** — open to anyone, any time commitment, no academic affiliation required.
- **Internship pathway** — for students seeking academic credit, formal experiential-learning hours, or recognized work experience through a school program.

Both pathways follow the same curriculum, mentorship model, and standards. The differences are in documentation, evaluation, and coordination with academic institutions, described below.

## Program Purpose

The program exists to give engineers — early-career, students, in-transition, returning-to-tech, or simply curious about mission-driven work — the opportunity to **build new skills and grow as engineers** in a real, modern, production-grade environment, while contributing to a nonprofit cause they care about.

This program is, first and foremost, **for the participant**. It is structured around your learning and professional development. You set the pace, you choose how much to commit, and the work, mentorship, and feedback are designed to help you grow. The Buy Nothing Project benefits incidentally from contributions made along the way, but the primary goal of this program — and the reason it exists — is to invest in participants as engineers and as people.

Participants receive no compensation, salary, stipend, or benefits, and have no expectation of paid employment. Participation is voluntary, at-will on both sides, and may be ended by either party at any time.

## About the Project

The Buy Nothing Project is a Public Benefit Corporation (PBC, Registered in Delaware) operating a global gift-economy platform with over 1.7 million users. Our community gives, receives, lends, and shares freely with neighbors — no money, no barter, no strings.

We chose to invest in a learning program because we believe the same generosity that animates our community should extend to how we develop the next generation of engineers. We're a small full-time engineering team, which means participants get unusually direct access to mentorship, code review, and a real production codebase — the kinds of learning experiences that are often hard to come by.

## Skills and Outcomes You Can Expect to Develop

The program is designed to give you concrete, transferable skills that strengthen your career regardless of where you go next. Over the course of your participation, you can expect to build experience in:

- Reading and reasoning about a real production codebase, not a tutorial project
- Submitting work through professional code review and responding constructively to feedback
- Writing tests, handling edge cases, and shipping changes that hold up under real user load
- Working with modern engineering practices: CI/CD, version control workflows, infrastructure-as-code, observability, security-first design
- Collaborating asynchronously across a distributed team
- Estimating, scoping, and communicating about engineering work
- Understanding how engineering decisions affect users at scale (1.7M of them)

You'll leave the program a stronger engineer than you arrived. That is the point.

## What's Expected of You

Because this is a learning program, the expectations are framed around engagement and growth, not productivity targets:

- **Show up for your own learning.** Come to your weekly 1:1, ask questions, take feedback seriously.
- **Communicate openly.** If something is unclear, blocked, or not working, say so early. Async communication is a learnable skill, and we'll help you build it.
- **Honor your commitments.** When you take on a ticket, finish it or communicate when you can't. Following through is itself a professional skill we're helping you develop.
- **Treat people well.** Respect, honesty, and care in all interactions — with full-time staff, other participants, and the community.
- **Take security seriously.** You'll be working in a system with real user data. We'll teach you the practices, and you're expected to follow them.

You set your own pace. The program adapts to your availability, not the other way around.

## Tiers and Time Commitment

You choose a tier based on what you can realistically commit to, and you can move between tiers as your circumstances change. We'd rather you pick the smaller tier and exceed it than overcommit and burn out.

| Tier | Hours/week | Learning focus | Typical pathway |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Casual** | 4–10 | Building familiarity with a production codebase through bug fixes, small isolated improvements, documentation, and tests | Volunteer |
| **Regular** | 10–25 | Developing feature-engineering skills under active mentor pairing | Volunteer or part-time intern |
| **Intern** | 25–40 | Practicing end-to-end ownership of small features, including design, implementation, review, and follow-through | Volunteer or full-time intern (often for academic credit) |

There's no fixed program length. Volunteer participation is open-ended; internships are typically scoped to a semester, quarter, or summer term, but the structure adapts to the academic calendar of your institution. The relationship is at-will on both sides, and there's no penalty for leaving when life requires it.

## Internship Pathway: Academic Credit and Recognition

We actively support participants whose schools offer academic credit, experiential-learning hours, or formal work-experience recognition for technical internships. If your program requires documentation, evaluation, or coordination with the Organization, we will work with you and your institution to meet those requirements. Specifically, we can:

- **Sign your school's internship affiliation or learning agreement** (subject to reasonable review by us and our counsel)
- **Define documented learning objectives** at the start of the term, mapped to your school's requirements
- **Provide mid-term and end-of-term evaluations** in the format your school requires
- **Track and verify hours** to whatever level of granularity your program needs
- **Honor your academic calendar** — exam periods, breaks, and term boundaries take priority over engineering work
- **Provide a final letter or certificate** documenting your participation, role, hours, and contributions
- **Communicate with your faculty advisor or internship coordinator** as needed throughout the term

To enable this, we'll need from your school: any required affiliation agreement, the program's learning-objective and evaluation forms, and the contact information for your faculty advisor or internship office.

If your school does not formally recognize external internships, you are still welcome to apply through the volunteer pathway and we will provide informal documentation of your experience.

## What You'll Work On

Tasks are selected for their **learning value to the participant**, not for what's most operationally urgent for the organization. That's a deliberate design choice. A ticket isn't given to a participant because it needs to ship — it's given because it's a good vehicle for the participant to develop a particular skill, with a mentor available to coach through it.

Typical learning-focused work includes:

- Bug investigation and fixes (a great way to learn a codebase)
- Feature work scoped for skill development
- Tests, tooling, and documentation
- Performance, observability, and reliability improvements

Every ticket assigned to a participant is pre-scoped: clear acceptance criteria, a designated mentor, and a definition of done. If a ticket isn't pre-scoped to that level, it isn't appropriate for the program. For interns whose academic program requires a defined project, we can scope a multi-ticket project at the start of the term that maps to your learning objectives.

## Onboarding (Two-Week Ramp)

Same ramp regardless of pathway or tier. The onboarding is itself part of the learning experience.

**Before access (Day 0).** Signed legal pack: Volunteer Agreement & Code of Conduct, IP Assignment Agreement, NDA. (For interns: any required school affiliation agreement and learning-objectives document.) 2FA confirmed on all accounts. GitHub handle on file.

**Days 1–3: Read-only orientation.** Architecture overview, codebase tour (recorded for you to revisit), 30-minute kickoff with the program lead to align on your goals and what you want to learn.

**Days 4–7: First PR.** Staging access provisioned. You take a "warm-up" task — chosen for its instructional value — and your mentor pairs with you on the review.

**Week 2: Full ramp.** Tier-appropriate access granted, first real ticket, weekly 1:1s begin.

## Mentorship and Feedback

You'll have two points of contact dedicated to your development:

- A **program lead** who owns your overall experience, runs your weekly 1:1, helps you set learning goals, and clears blockers. For interns, the program lead is your designated site supervisor.
- A **rotating reviewer** — one full-time engineer per week is "on call" for participant PRs and questions. The reviewer rotates so you get exposure to multiple engineers' perspectives and styles.

Code review in this program is explicitly framed as teaching, not gatekeeping. You should expect detailed, constructive feedback — and you're encouraged to push back, ask why, and learn the reasoning behind the standards we hold.

Most communication is async (Slack, PR comments, ticket threads). Sync time is reserved for kickoffs, 1:1s, and unblocking conversations.

## Access and Security (and Why It Matters for Your Learning)

The platform holds real user data, so our access posture is conservative — and learning to work within those constraints is itself a valuable professional skill.

- **GitHub:** Team-scoped membership, branch protection, signed commits, required reviews from CODEOWNERS.
- **AWS:** Per-participant IAM role via SSO. No long-lived keys.
- **Staging:** Read/write access for your development work.
- **Production:** Read-only, with PII fields excluded and explicit denies on networking, IAM, and secrets. Participants do not make changes to live production services. Production changes are reserved for full-time engineers.
- **Secrets:** Staging-only scope. Participants never see real production secrets.
- **Offboarding:** Access is revoked within 24 hours of program exit, by either party.

Working within a real security posture — with proper IAM, least-privilege access, and clear separation between staging and production — is something you'd otherwise only learn at a security-conscious employer. It's part of the curriculum.

## Code of Conduct

The Buy Nothing Project's values — generosity, mutual aid, equity, care for community and planet — apply to how we treat each other. The full Code of Conduct is in the Volunteer Agreement. The short version: treat every person with respect, communicate honestly, listen well, and prioritize repair over defensiveness when you make a mistake. Harassment, discrimination, and retaliation against good-faith reporters are not tolerated, and will end participation in the program.

## Legal Paperwork

Before you write a single line of code, three documents need to be signed:

1. **Volunteer Agreement & Code of Conduct** — the terms of the participant relationship. (The same agreement applies to both pathways; "Volunteer" in the document is used in its broad sense to include unpaid interns.)
2. **Intellectual Property Assignment Agreement** — work created during the program belongs to the Organization. (This is standard for nonprofit programs and is not unusual.)
3. **Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement** — protects user data, infrastructure details, and other confidential information.

Interns may also need to sign their school's affiliation agreement, which we'll review and sign on our end if its terms are reasonable.

We'll send these as part of the offer. You're encouraged to have your own counsel review them. Take the time you need; we'd rather you sign with full understanding than with reservations.

## After the Program

The skills, references, and portfolio work you build here are yours, and they go with you wherever you go next. We'll provide:

- A written confirmation of your dates and contributions, on request
- A reference and recommendation, where earned, from the program lead
- Specific examples of work you can discuss in future interviews (subject to confidentiality boundaries)
- For interns: a final evaluation in your school's required format and any additional documentation needed for academic credit

There is no guarantee of full-time employment with the Buy Nothing Project, and the program is not structured as a hiring funnel. If a paid role happens to open while you're in the program, you'd be welcome to apply on the same footing as any other candidate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is this an internship?**
It can be. The program supports both open-ended volunteer participation and structured internships for academic credit or recognized work experience. Both pathways follow the same curriculum and mentorship model — the difference is in documentation, evaluation, and coordination with your school.

**Will I get paid for any of this?**
No. The program is a learning and developmental experience, not paid work. Participants receive no compensation, no stipend, and no benefits. Pre-approved out-of-pocket expenses can be reimbursed.

**Will my school accept this for credit?**
That depends on your school's program. We're set up to work with schools that recognize external technical internships: we can sign affiliation agreements, provide evaluations, track hours, and coordinate with your faculty advisor. The decision on whether to award credit rests with your school; we'll provide whatever documentation they require.

**Do I have to be a student to participate?**
No. The volunteer pathway is open to anyone, regardless of student status. The internship pathway is for participants whose schools formally recognize the experience.

**Why does the project run a program like this if it doesn't get cheap labor out of it?**
Because investing in engineers — especially those who are early-career, transitioning, returning to tech, or building toward their first role — fits the values of the Buy Nothing Project. Mentorship and skill development are themselves a form of giving.

**Do I need to be in a specific timezone?**
No. Most work is async. We do ask for some overlap with US working hours for your weekly 1:1.

**Do I need professional experience?**
Not necessarily. The program is explicitly designed to be accessible to engineers building toward their first or next role. Strong portfolio work, open-source contributions, course projects, or a CS background are all reasonable starting points.

**What stack will I be working in?**
Node.js, Typescript, ReactNative, SQL, ORM, Terraform, AWS, GitHub, Jira. Adjacent experience is fine if you're willing to ramp.  Use of AI/LLM Coding partners is encouraged!

**Can I list this on my resume / LinkedIn?**
Yes. Typical titles: "Volunteer Software Engineer, Buy Nothing Project" or "Software Engineering Intern, Buy Nothing Project" depending on pathway. We'll provide written confirmation of your dates and contributions on request.

**Can I contribute work to my own open-source projects?**
Work created during the program belongs to the Organization. Contributing program work product to outside open-source projects requires written approval. Independent personal projects, created on your own time without Organization resources, remain entirely yours.
